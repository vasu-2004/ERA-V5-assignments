How attention learned to pay less

Vanilla attention was never wrong. It was expensive. Every mechanism below is someone looking at that bill and deciding which part of it they were no longer willing to pay — compute, memory, exactness, or reach. Eighteen mechanisms, in the order they actually shipped.

01The bill

Four things get expensive at once as a transformer scales, and every mechanism on this page is a trade against one or more of them.

ComputeThe Q·K² score matrix is O(n²) — 1M tokens is a trillion multiply-adds, once, per layer.
MemoryThe KV cache grows with every generated token. 1M-token context for a handful of concurrent users is measured in terabytes of GPU RAM.
PositionAttention has no innate sense of order. Something has to tell Q and K how far apart two tokens are — and whether that still works past training length.
ExactnessSoftmax gives an exact, fresh, competitive ranking over every past token every time. Anything that speeds this up risks throwing that away.

Read the cards below in date order, not by family. The instructor's point — and it holds up — is that grouping them by type hides the actual conversation the field has been having with itself since 2017. A list can't show you that. A timeline can.

04Sources & methodology

Every date on this page is an arXiv v1 submission date, a dated blog/model release, or — for the one mechanism that started as a community post rather than a paper — the original public post, checked directly against the source page (title, ID, and date), not against a training-data recollection. Full citation table with links is in the repository README.md. Two judgment calls worth being explicit about:

“Absolute learned positions” is dated to GPT-1 (Jun 2018), the first influential model to make trained-per-slot position vectors the standard approach, rather than to the original Transformer paper, whose appendix mentions testing a learned variant as a footnote to its real proposal (sinusoidal).  “Sliding window attention” is dated to Longformer (Apr 2020), which named and formalized the mechanism, not to the 2019 Sparse Transformer, whose strided/fixed patterns are closely related but distinct (and are credited separately, above, as the sparse/top-k entry).
